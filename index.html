Methodology and data notes
  • Component 1, data-sparsity-adjusted safety stock. Classical: SS = z × √(L·σD² + D²·σL²). ACRF: SS = z·c(n) × √(L·σD² + D²·σL²), with c(n) = ln(1+n) / ln(1+730), bounded to [0.5, 1.0]. D and σD come from daily demand per category (days with no orders count as zero); L and σL from actual shipping days. n is the number of days in the observation window.
  • Component 2, iterative forecast-error correction. A moving-average forecast (default 5 days) is scored with a rolling MAPE (default 30-day window). The latest MAPE sets a correction factor max(0.8, 1 − MAPE/200) that multiplies c(n) when feedback is switched on.
  • Component 3, Composite Capacity-Risk Score. 40% utilization efficiency (share of deliveries on or before schedule), 35% forecast reliability (100 − rolling MAPE), and 25% disruption exposure ((1 − HHI) × 150, using shipping mode as a proxy for carrier diversification). 70+ Low, 50–69 Moderate, below 50 High risk.
  • Capital not tied up = (classical − ACRF safety stock) × average unit price per category.
  • Sample data. DataCo SMART Supply Chain for Big Data Analysis (Constante, Silva & Pereira, Mendeley Data, 2019, doi:10.17632/8gx2fvg2k6.5), filtered to the U.S. Consumer segment: 12,432 order records from April 1 to August 25, 2016. The default view uses the five highest-volume categories (7,596 records). Download the sample file.
  • This is a demonstration of the framework's calculation logic on public data, not an outcome-based comparison of realized stockouts or holding costs.
